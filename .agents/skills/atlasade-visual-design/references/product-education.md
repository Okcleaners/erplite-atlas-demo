# Interactive product education

## Choose a question a merchant can settle

Start with a decision and a plausible mistaken prediction. Ask what would change if the visitor varied one thing, and which reference value should stay fixed. Write the answer in plain language first. If reading that sentence settles the whole question, a worked example may be enough. An experiment earns its space when comparing two outcomes reveals why they differ.

Place the question beside a labeled control and a recognizable record. Offer a prediction without making the visitor pass a quiz. Let them vary one bounded input, see the consequence, then open the arithmetic or record detail that explains it. Preserve their input, record identity and focus when they open the explanation. Provide a way to return to the starting case. End with a fresh case they can reason through without a hint.

## Keep the business model inspectable

Identify the synthetic fixture, authoritative transition, units, rounding rule and scope. Calculate every displayed business value from the same deterministic scenario state, including labels in the diagram, spoken feedback and worked arithmetic. Treat a proposed variation beyond the existing fixture as a proposed model. Keep illustrative assumptions visible and cite approved public wording for any claim about the merchant product.

Distinguish editable what-if inputs, a committed event, and inspection of that event. Compute each alternative from the same starting snapshot. A repeat click, focus change, explanation, animation or perspective switch must never post an additional movement. Verify baseline, changed input, invalid input and reset. Show an unchanged comparison where it teaches the boundary.

| Merchant question | An informative comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Which shelf lost the item? | Follow a sold line to stock at its selling location. Keep a second location's count visible as a control. |
| What stayed in the drawer? | Compare tender received and change returned with the net movement for the same receipt. Keep tax distinct from sales. |
| What does attaching a customer change? | Identify the associated ticket and show whether the fixture changes its amount. Give a customer link a different label from a money movement. |
| What changes when a business adds a location? | In a labeled concept, compare a shared product identity with separate store counts. State which business rules are assumptions. |
| How might an operational record inform ERP reporting? | Show the documented relationship or a labeled conceptual mapping. Actual delivery, synchronization and commercial availability each require approved evidence. |

## Make the change readable

Use native form controls and semantic HTML for inputs, records and amounts. An optional local SVG can trace a labeled relationship once. Align input, result and explanation in reading order. On a phone keep the input and its immediate result close together. A disclosure can hold the longer proof. Provide a keyboard and touch alternative to dragging, preserve focus, and announce one settled result. Reduced motion and a static worked example must convey the same causal relationship.

Use the [visual qualification checks](qualification.md) for matched screenshots, measured interaction latency and payload. Include both the changed and invariant records in the comparison. Ask a first-time visitor to predict a new case and explain the outcome in their own words. Task completion alone cannot establish comprehension. Record which results were calculated, tested with a visitor, or proposed.

Use HTML, CSS and SVG for records, quantities and flows. Consider advanced rendering such as Three.js only for a named learning task that loses essential information in a simpler representation. Document the simpler alternative and the observable difference a learner would gain. Obtain separate approval before implementation. Isolate and lazy-load the renderer on demand, retain a complete semantic fallback, and qualify incremental bytes, interaction latency and low-end mobile behavior against the simpler baseline.
